## Standard Chartered to cut around 15% of corporate functions roles by 2030

Standard Chartered has unveiled plans to axe around 15% of its corporate functions roles by 2030 as the bank accelerates its integration of AI and pursues several growth targets.

The planned reductions will impact approximately 7,800 of the bank's more than 52,000 corporate functions employees over the next four years.

The bank says the changes form part of a strategic shift toward what it describes as a "simpler, faster and more connected operating model" designed to support its next phase of expansion.

In comments to the media reported by Reuters, group CEO Bill Winters confirms that the restructuring will primarily impact back-office operations at centres in Bangalore, Chennai, Kuala Lumpur, and Warsaw.

In a separate development announced this week, Standard Chartered has also agreed to acquire the custody business of Zodia Custody, pending regulatory approval.

## Mercury bags $200m Series D at $5.2bn valuation

Business banking fintech Mercury has raised $200 million in a Series D funding round led by TCV with participation from Andreessen Horowitz, Coatue, CRV, Sapphire Ventures, Sequoia Capital, and Spark Capital.

The round values Mercury at $5.2 billion, up 48.6% on the company's previous $3.5 billion valuation secured following its Series C funding round last March.

Having received conditional approval for a US national bank charter in April, Mercury is now passing through the bank organisation phase and is expected to use its new funding to fuel its journey towards becoming a fully regulated national bank.

## A2A fintech Dwolla sold to NMI

US-headquartered fintech NMI is developing its flagship embedded payments platform beyond payment acceptance to include bank‑payment rails and enhanced money movement infrastructure through the acquisition of account‑to‑account (A2A) payments provider Dwolla. The financial terms of the deal have not been disclosed.

Dwolla operates payment APIs that let businesses move money between US bank accounts using domestic real-time networks like Automated Clearing House (ACH) and Real-Time Payments (RTP). The sale to NMI comes after founder Ben Milne departed Dwolla last August after 17 years of leadership.

The buyout includes contracts with 400+ Dwolla customers and will see approximately 60 Dwolla employees join NMI. Dave Glaser, who has served as CEO of Dwolla since September 2023, will move to become chief operating officer, serving under new NMI CEO Steve Pinado.

## Banking Circle names former Mastercard exec Kush Saxena CEO as co-founders step back

Banking Circle, a Luxembourg-based B2B banking services fintech, has named former Mastercard exec Kush Saxena as the group's new CEO, effective immediately.

Saxena's appointment marks a significant leadership transition at Banking Circle, with co-founders Anders la Cour and Laust Bertelsen stepping back from the company they established in 2013.

La Cour, who Saxena directly succeeds as group CEO, has already transitioned to a senior adviser role, while Bertelsen will continue as CEO of the fintech's banking subsidiary, Banking Circle S.A., through to the end of April 2027.

## Citi and HPS Investment Partners launch €15bn private capital programme

Citi has inked a new partnership with BlackRock's alternative investment management subsidiary, HPS Investment Partners, to create a €15 billion private capital programme for businesses.

The programme will see HPS provide debt financing to firms with principal business based in Continental Europe and the UK. There are additional plans to expand the programme's pool of potential borrowers to the Middle East, though no timeline for this has been provided.

Citi says it will "leverage its extensive investment, corporate, and commercial bank-led origination capabilities to source investment opportunities" for the programme, adding: "Eligible opportunities will comprise a broad range of sub-investment grade debt instruments."
