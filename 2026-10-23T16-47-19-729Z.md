## Barclays to axe around 5,000 jobs this year

UK-based banking giant Barclays is cutting around 5,000 jobs this year amid a wider push to drive efficiency savings and bring down costs. The cuts will represent around 5% of the bank's total workforce.

Reports state that Barclays has already reduced its headcount by 1,000 in 2026, with the remaining 4,000 cuts set to take place before the end of the year.

Announcing the planned headcount reduction in the bank's Q3 earnings presentation, Barclays CEO CS Venkatakrishnan said 60% of the cuts "relate to support functions, including fewer developers as AI makes our people more productive".

## LSEG to acquire Octus in $3.5bn all-cash deal

The London Stock Exchange Group (LSEG) has agreed to purchase US-based credit intelligence platform Octus from investment firm Permira and Octus' management stakeholders in an all-cash deal valued at $3.535 billion.

Founded in 2013 by Kent Collier, Octus provides more than 750 institutional customers and 40,000 individuals from investment banks, law firms, advisory firms, and buy-side organisations with credit and data analytics tools.

In a joint statement, the companies say: "Combining Octus’ capabilities with LSEG’s fixed income data, analytics and distribution means customers will benefit from integrated solutions and intelligence across public and private credit enabling them to source deals, assess risk and manage their portfolios through LSEG’s platform."

## Ramp applies for US national trust bank charter

Expense management fintech Ramp has submitted an application for a national trust bank charter to the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC).

If approved, the fintech plans to establish Ramp National Trust Bank, offering customers "fiduciary investment management and custody of digital assets", according to a LinkedIn post by Ramp announcing the application.

The LinkedIn post continues: "The proposed trust bank would bring these services under a consistent federal regulatory framework." The company adds that the proposed bank will "not take deposits or make commercial loans".

## NAB plans to cut 2,500 jobs in FY27

National Australia Bank (NAB) has announced that it expects to "reduce its internal workforce by up to 2,500 roles or ~6% in FY27" as part of an operational restructure.

The statement notes that the workforce reduction will include "up to 1,500 roles before the end of 2026".

Additionally, NAB says it will "materially reduce its use of consultants and external contractors".

NAB says it has undertaken this restructure "to simplify its operating model and better align its workforce to prioritise growth and technology initiatives for customers".

## US fintech Whop lands $200m Series D at $5.1bn valuation

US-based fintech Whop has raised $200 million in a Series D funding round led by Sequoia Capital, with participation from Shopify Ventures.

The round values the company at $5.1 billion. The Series D marks Whop's second raise of the year, building on a $200 million investment from Tether in February at a $1.6 billion valuation.

Founded in 2021 by CEO Steven Schwartz, CGO Cameron Zoub, and CTO Jack Sharkey, Whop is a fintech platform that helps entrepreneurs start and scale their businesses. The platform provides the infrastructure to run their own digital storefront, including accepting fiat and digital payments, sending invoices, and automating services with AI.
