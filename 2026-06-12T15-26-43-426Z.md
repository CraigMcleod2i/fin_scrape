## HSBC, Lloyds, and NatWest back UK’s first sovereign AI model

London-based AI start-up Cosine has aligned with a coalition of UK financial institutions to co-design Lumen Sovereign, which is being pitched as Britain's first sovereign frontier AI model.

Babcock International Group, BT, Lloyds Banking Group, LSEG, NatWest Group, HSBC, PwC, Thales UK, Leonardo UK, BAE Systems, and Telefónica Tech UK&I have all inked memorandums of understanding to support the model's design phase.

Alistair Pullen, who leads Cosine as CEO, says the project is "building a model that is fully trained on UK soil and available [in] air gapped environments", and promises "a far more efficient price point than OpenAI and Anthropic alternatives” when the model goes live, with deployment readiness forecast by the end of 2026.

## Intesa Sanpaolo launches $35bn bid for Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena

Italy's Intesa Sanpaolo has launched a bid to acquire domestic rival Banca Monte dei Paschi di Siena (MPS) in a deal valued at approximately €30 billion (around $35 billion), representing what would be the largest transaction in Italian banking history.

According to a statement from Intesa: "This offer has the strategic objective of further strengthening the European leadership of Intesa Sanpaolo in wealth management, protection and advisory and the sustainability of value creation for all stakeholders".

The deal will create a combined institution with approximately €1.7 trillion in assets, serving over 27 million clients and operating roughly 3,000 branches nationwide.

## Temenos acquires Additiv to build out wealth offering

Switzerland-headquartered core banking technology company Temenos has acquired local Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) fintech provider Additiv to further build out its Temenos Wealth offering.

Additiv operates technology that enables banks, insurers, asset and wealth managers, and other non-financial institutions to launch digital banking, credit, insurance, and wealth products.

Temenos says it's acquiring 100% of the business through equal parts of cash and equity, and expects to close the transaction in early Q3 2026, subject to customary closing conditions.

Once completed, Additiv will operate as a standalone business, with its 200-person team continuing to be led by founder Michael Stemmle and Nils Frowein, who has served as CEO since July 2023.

## Digital Asset lands $355m in a16z crypto-led funding round

Software company Digital Asset has secured $355 million in a new funding round to drive the next phase of growth for its public Layer-1 blockchain, Canton Network.

The funding round was led by Andreessen Horowitz through its a16z crypto fund. Major financial institutions backing the raise include HSBC, BNP Paribas, ABN Amro, Broadridge, SoFi Technologies, Tradeweb, S&P Global, and the Abu Dhabi Investment Authority.

This first-time investment from Andreessen Horowitz, valued at $100 million, sparks a new collaboration between Digital Asset and the $90 billion-asset venture capital firm.

In a statement announcing the raise, Digital Asset says this collaboration will provide "access to a16z crypto's expertise across company building, crypto, policy, and research as Canton scales and the ecosystem expands".

## iAltA consolidates recent wealthtech acquisitions to launch Field

WestCap-backed private markets infrastructure firm iAltA Holdings has combined two recent acquisitions to create a new wealth management technology company called Field.

Field is the result of iAltA bringing together technology from BridgeFT and Precept. BridgeFT is a Wealthtech-as-a-Service platform acquired by iAltA in January, while the purchase of integration platform Precept followed in April.

The technologies now work together under the Field brand, which is designed as an operational database that consolidates back-office records, client accounts, and custodian data for advisory firms specifically.
