## Bertelsmann to establish Riverty Bank with new EU licence

Buy now, pay later (BNPL) fintech Riverty is setting up a new banking entity in Luxembourg, having finalised a 10-month regulatory process to secure an EU CRR banking licence from the European Central Bank and Luxembourg's national financial regulator, CSSF.

The Baden-Baden-headquartered company has selected its current chief risk officer, Oliver Kuhaupt, to lead the newly-minted Riverty Bank, which plans to commence operations in July 2026.

The regulatory green light upgrades Riverty from a standard PSD2 payment fintech to a fully-licensed credit institution, allowing it to offer embedded payment, credit, and liquidity services directly to European merchants using its own balance sheet.

## Mastercard lands New York BitLicense for blockchain payments

Mastercard has landed BitLicense approval with the New York State Department of Financial Services (NYDFS), enabling the global network to conduct digital asset business in the state.

The approval will be used to support a long-term strategy for building and operating payment and settlement infrastructure that can support digital currencies including stablecoins and tokenised deposits.

Mastercard has already made significant headway in facilitating digital assets through its network, namely via its acquisition of London-based stablecoin platform BVNK for up to $1.8 billion in March.

## Government of Georgia selects Tether to launch national stablecoin

The Government of Georgia has partnered with El Salvador-based stablecoin issuer Tether to launch a Georgian Lari stablecoin, GELT.

In a statement announcing the partnership, Tether says that GELT will support cross-border commerce with near-instant digital payments and "lower transaction costs". The statement continues that the stablecoin will provide "broader access to programmable financial infrastructure throughout Georgia and the wider region".

The stablecoin issuer concludes: "Further details regarding GELT's structure, rollout, and regulatory implementation will be announced at a later stage."

## Monzo Mobile to launch this summer

Monzo is expanding beyond digital banking services with the arrival of Monzo Mobile, which the UK challenger has achieved through an exclusive multi-year partnership with Virgin Media O2, and expects to roll out this summer.

The debut mobile plan and eSIM offering is aimed at helping Monzo's 14 million personal customers "stay connected and manage mobile spending", according to a statement, with in-app features for tracking data usage, roaming, and mobile spend.

Three different plans are to be made available through Virgin Media O2, which Monzo says is providing the underlying UK mobile network infrastructure in partnership with eSIM specialist 1GLOBAL.

## Catena files for national trust bank charter, secures $30m in Series A funding

US-based agentic AI-focused fintech Catena has secured $30 million in Series A funding, building on the $18 million it raised in 2025.

Founded in 2022 by former Circle SVP of product engineering Matt Venables and Circle co-founder and board member Sean Neville, Catena says it is building a regulated financial institution designed for AI agents and their operators.

Agents will be given the ability to open accounts, hold money, and send payments via stablecoins and cards, all in line with human-defined and monitored policies. Catena states that it will create an immutable audit trail, which shows the agents’ intent, evaluation, and execution.

Alongside the new funding, Catena adds it has submitted its application for a national trust bank charter. According to Catena’s website, the firm says it is “pursuing OCC-regulated charter status to provide direct, governed banking for agents”.
