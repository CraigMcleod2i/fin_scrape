## Bank of America hit with $1.21bn in penalties by US regulators

Bank of America has been hit with $1.21 billion in penalties by US regulators over alleged violations of national anti-money laundering and sanctions laws.

According to the US Department of the Treasury, FinCEN assessed a $1.2 billion penalty for "the bank’s failure to mitigate and report priority national security risks, including Chinese Money Laundering Networks and illicit finance along the southwest border".

This fine includes a $750 million civil money penalty by the OCC - the largest in its history - over "the bank’s deficient and ineffective BSA/AML (anti-money laundering) compliance program", the Treasury continues.

The Treasury says on top of this, OFAC announced an $11.6 million settlement agreement with Bank of America over "apparent violations of numerous sanctions programs, including on Iran, Russia, narcotics, and global terrorism".

In a statement sent to FinTech Futures, a Bank of America spokesperson said: "We have invested billions of dollars in technology and dedicated thousands of employees to combat financial crime, and our team is far along with and committed to completing the work required to address the issues raised by our regulators."

## Lloyds names HSBC's Jane Gilmour as new CTO

Lloyds Banking Group has appointed banking veteran Jane Gilmour as its new chief technology officer (CTO), bringing more than three decades of technology leadership experience to the role.

Gilmour is set to join the bank in January 2027, moving from HSBC, where she has held the role of CTO for group functions technology (formerly enterprise technology) since August 2024.

Gilmour's appointment concludes Lloyds' search for a successor to Victor Weigler, who announced his retirement in April. Gilmour will oversee Lloyds' product roadmap and lead the digital overhaul supporting Accelerate 2030, the bank's four-year growth strategy.

## Payward to secure EU banking licence with Saldo Bank acquisition deal

Kraken parent company Payward Inc is acquiring Finland's Saldo Finance Oy in a move that will fit its new banking business, Payward Banking, with a specialised European banking licence. The financial terms of the agreed deal have not been made public, and the transaction is set to close in 2027, subject to regulatory approval.

At the centre of the acquisition is Saldo Bank, which currently operates term deposits, loans, and small business lending services in Lithuania, Finland, Sweden, the Netherlands, and Germany, with a banking licence first granted by the European Central Bank in 2021.

The bank and holding company will be integrated into the Payward Banking regulatory stack which currently includes a master account with the US Federal Reserve and an Electronic Money Institution licence from the Central Bank of Ireland.

Once the acquisition is completed, Payward Banking will expand its capabilities to include interest on deposits, as well as secured and unsecured small business loans.

## OCC fines American Express $350m

Sandy, Utah-headquartered American Express National Bank, a subsidiary of American Express, has been handed a $350 million civil money penalty alongside a cease-and-desist order by the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) for alleged "deficiencies in the bank’s Bank Secrecy Act (BSA) and anti-money laundering (AML) compliance program", according to a statement by the US watchdog.

In announcing the fine, the regulator says it "determined that the bank failed to establish and maintain a BSA/AML compliance program reasonably designed to assure and monitor compliance with the BSA and its implementing regulations".

The OCC alleges that the bank "experienced systemic breakdowns in its suspicious activity monitoring and reporting processes, resulting in a failure to timely identify, evaluate, and sufficiently report approximately $13 billion of suspected trade-based money laundering activity that took place over the past decade".

## Crédit Agricole to launch new digital retail bank in Germany, agrees to buy IKB

French banking group Crédit Agricole is working to expand its position in neighbouring Germany by consolidating the operations of regional subsidiary Creditplus into a new universal bank called Crédit Agricole Deutschland, while also signing a deal to acquire IKB Deutsche Industriebank from Lone Star Funds.

Crédit Agricole Deutschland will be a digital-first offering for German retail customers and will launch at the end of October. The initial offering will include current accounts with German IBANs, a Visa card programme, mobile app, and savings solutions featuring Tagesgeld and Festgeld deposits.

IKB Deutsche Industriebank is a commercial lender that has €14.3 billion in total assets and serves small and medium-sized enterprises and corporate clients in Germany.

Crédit Agricole states that the acquisition provides "immediate scale in the German mid-corporate banking market" through the lending, capital market, and advisory services offered by IKB Deutsche Industriebank.
