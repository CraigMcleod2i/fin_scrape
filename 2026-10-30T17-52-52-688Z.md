## Barclays appoints Anita Tanna as first chief AI officer

Barclays has appointed its group chief of staff Anita Tanna as its first chief AI officer. Announcing the news on LinkedIn, Tanna states that as AI chief, she will focus on accelerating the tech's adoption "across the group and focusing on where we can unlock the greatest value".

Tanna first joined the UK-headquartered banking group in 2021 as managing director and EMEA head of equity sales of Barclays Corporate and Investment Bank after spending close to 12 years working at Citi. She rose to group chief of staff and managing director in 2024.

"AI has enormous potential to transform how we serve our customers and clients and is a critical enabler of our strategy," Tanna writes.

## Wealthsimple raises around $300m at new $9.8bn valuation

Canadian money management firm Wealthsimple has secured an equity round of up to CAD $425 million (just over $300 million), at a post-money valuation of CAD $13.8 billion ($9.8 billion).

Singapore-based investor GIC has led the round, with participation from existing shareholders Inovia, Power Corporation of Canada, IGM Financial, ICONIQ, and Canada Pension Plan Investment Board (CPP Investments).

According to a company statement, the fresh cash injection will be used to further "accelerate its product roadmap", which includes "expanding its business, everyday spending and chequing, trading and investment products".

## Ripple secures final OCC authorisation to establish Ripple National Trust Bank

Ripple has received final approval from the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) to charter its new national trust bank, Ripple National Trust Bank (RNTB).

Under the OCC's supervision, RNTB will provide "fiduciary digital asset custody services" while also "managing the segregated reserves supporting Ripple USD (RLUSD)", Ripple's stablecoin, and "acting as collateral trustee of the reserves for the benefit of RLUSD holders", according to a company statement.

The approval comes 10 months after Ripple received its conditional approval from the US regulator in December. RLUSD will continue to be issued by Ripple's New York Department of Financial Services-regulated limited purpose trust company, Standard Custody & Trust Company, with BNY serving as the primary custodian.

## Metro Bank chair Robert Sharpe to step down

Robert Sharpe, chair of the board at Metro Bank, has informed the UK lender of his intention to step down when his current six-year term ends on 31 October 2026.

Sharpe will be succeeded by senior independent director Catherine Brown as interim chair of the board and nomination committee. Brown will in turn be succeeded by non-executive director Michael Torpey.

Both appointments remain subject to regulatory approval. "Together they will lead the search for a new chair," says Metro Bank.

In a statement, Sharpe describes his time at Metro Bank as "incredibly rewarding", noting that the bank has "returned to profitable growth" and is "well placed for a successful future", adding: "It's the right time for me to step away."

## Smartlayer founder launches new home financial management start-up MIZAN

Entrepreneur Tahir Farooqui has taken to LinkedIn to announce the launch of his new venture MIZAN, a home management start-up aiming to improve the relationship between homeowners and financial institutions.

Packaged as a white-label solution for banks, insurers, and landlords, MIZAN is designed to keep an up-to-date record of home ownership, consolidating everything from mortgage and insurance to bills and repairs in one place.

The rollout will initially take place across Germany, where the company is headquartered, and the UK. The company is still in the negotiation phase with insurers in the UK and large residential landlords in Germany.

To date, the start-up has largely been bootstrapped by Farooqui himself, though he tells FinTech Futures that ahead of its institutional rollout, the company is preparing a $2 million pre-seed raise.
