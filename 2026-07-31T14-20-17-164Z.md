## HSBC sells Singapore life and health insurance business to Allianz for €2bn

Allianz has agreed to acquire HSBC's health and life insurance business in Singapore for €2 billion (SGD 2.9 billion). The sale of HSBC Life Singapore to Allianz Europe BV is expected to close in H1 2027, subject to approvals from the Monetary Authority of Singapore.

The acquisition includes all existing HSBC Life Singapore staff, client relationships, and an exclusive distribution partnership under which Allianz will provide protection, health, retirement, and wealth solutions to HSBC’s customers in Singapore.

A joint acquisition announcement states that the business generated an operating profit of €80 million for HSBC in 2025. HSBC expects to generate a pre-tax gain of $1.8 billion through the sale. Allianz forecasts "double-digit return on investment in the mid-term".

## OCC denies Wise's US national trust bank charter application

The US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) has denied global payments company Wise's application for a US national trust bank charter.

In its decision letter sent last week, the OCC notes that one month following Wise's application, the company's US subsidiary became subject to a public Multistate Consent Order. This order related to alleged "deficiencies in its Bank Secrecy Act, Anti-Money Laundering and Countering the Financing of Terrorism (AML/CFT) programme", states the regulator.

Specifically, the OCC claims the order identified "deficiencies related to Wise US’s processes for investigating and reporting suspicious activity, transaction monitoring data integrity concerns, and failure to timely file suspicious activity reports". Wise US subsequently agreed to pay a $4.2 million administrative fine.

Wise originally submitted its application to the OCC in June 2025, proposing to establish Wise National Trust (WNT) to provide stored-value accounts with debit cards to US customers, alongside fiduciary services.

## Visa to axe around 2,600 jobs as part of 7% workforce reduction

Payments giant Visa is set to axe around 7% of its workforce, with the cuts impacting roughly 2,600 positions across the company's over 34,000-employee base. Bloomberg reports that the job cuts will mainly impact tech and product roles.

According to a staff memo seen by Bloomberg, CEO Ryan McInerney writes: "To capture the opportunities ahead and best position Visa to lead this transformation, we must continue evolving how we work." McInerney adds that AI is "helping to accelerate this evolution and shape the way work gets done at Visa".

Bloomberg reports that a source familiar with the situation says Visa's long-term plan involves funnelling resources into consumer payments, commercial money-movement networks, and value-added products such as stablecoins, B2B platforms, and cross-border solutions.

## Social media platform X begins rollout of X Money in US

Elon Musk's social media company X has officially begun rolling out its payments platform X Money to customers in the US.

Premium and Premium+ subscribers on X will now have access to the X Money digital wallet, which features pay-in and pay-out capabilities enabling users to make peer-to-peer (P2P) payments, send wires, and pay bills within the X platform.

In a statement, X Money notes: "Deposit accounts are held at Cross River Bank, Member FDIC, and insured up to $250,000. Deposits are automatically enrolled in a cash sweep program, which provides up to $10 million of aggregate FDIC pass-through deposit insurance coverage, across participating FDIC-insured network banks."

In January 2024, X officially announced plans to launch P2P payment services within the app. A year later, X Money confirmed a partnership with Visa, marking the payments processor as its first major partner.

## Temenos rehires Tony Coleman as CTO amid new leadership appointments

Temenos has named Tony Coleman as chief technology officer (CTO), Cormac Flanagan as chief product officer (CPO), and Sriraman Ganesan as global head of product engineering.

The new team members will assume their roles on 3 August, joining the firm at the same time as incoming chief financial officer Daniel Schmucki, who was appointed to the role in April.

The appointments are part of a broader organisational restructure of Temenos' leadership team. Previously, the technology and product divisions were led by chief product and technology officer (CPTO) Barb Morgan, with former CTO Rohit Chauhan and former CPO Sairam Rangachari reporting to her.

With the announcement of the new executives, Temenos confirmed that Morgan has stepped down from her role. In lieu of a new CPTO, the firm has split the role, appointing a new CTO and CPO.

It is understood that Chauhan and Rangachari have departed the firm as neither is listed on Temenos' leadership page.
