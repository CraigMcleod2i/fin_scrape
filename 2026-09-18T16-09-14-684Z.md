## HSBC group CFO Pam Kaur to step down in 2027

HSBC's group chief financial officer (CFO) and executive director Pam Kaur has announced that she is leaving the bank in 2027.

In a form sent to the Securities and Exchange Commission, HSBC states that Kaur's "official retirement date as group CFO will be confirmed in due course but will be no later than the company’s 2027 AGM".

Upon stepping down from the executive position, Kaur will transition to an advisory role to the group CEO, which is expected to continue through to the end of 2028.

The board has begun the search for Kaur's successor and is considering both internal and external candidates.

## Saudi Arabia's Tabby raises $233m at $6.5bn valuation

Saudi Arabia-based fintech Tabby has secured $233 million in Series F funding at a valuation of $6.5 billion. The round was led by Blue Pool Capital with participation from existing backers Arbor Ventures, Wellington Management, and HSG.

Tabby's new market value almost doubles its $3.3 billion valuation from its $160 million Series E fundraise in February 2025.

The Series F comes only a few months after the fintech landed new Saudi consumer and business financing licences.

The regulatory approvals have enabled Tabby to offer longer Shariah-compliant payment plans to individuals as well as provide working capital to businesses on its platform.

## Enova scraps proposed Grasshopper Bank acquisition

Chicago, US-based online lender Enova says it has "withdrawn its applications with the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency and the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System related to the proposed acquisition of Grasshopper Bancorp", 10 months after it agreed to purchase the New York-headquartered bank.

Enova and Grasshopper had agreed a $369 million acquisition deal made up of stocks and cash in December that would have seen Grasshopper become the bank subsidiary of the new Enova bank holding company.

In a statement, Enova CEO Steve Cunningham says withdrawing the applications is the "best decision for Enova and our shareholders" and that the firm's future growth and success "do not depend on becoming a bank".

Cunningham continues: "Bank regulatory guidelines and attitudes have not kept pace with the realities of meeting the credit needs of tens of millions of consumers and small businesses underserved by traditional banks.

"Regulators do not have clear standards for nonbanks that want to become banks and that serve customers whose credit needs today are met mostly outside of the banking system. Without clearly articulated standards, the process is susceptible to political pressure and outside advocacy, rather than being guided strictly by the statutory factors that should govern it."

## Grab to acquire majority stake in BNPL provider Atome Financial for $1.49bn

Singapore-based super-app Grab has agreed to acquire a 60% majority stake in buy now, pay later (BNPL) company Atome Financial for $1.49 billion in cash as it looks to expand its financial services portfolio.

The proposed transaction is expected to close by Q3 2027, subject to regulatory approvals and closing conditions, and includes $260 million of primary growth capital.

The majority stake purchase is just phase one of the deal struck between Grab and the selling parties, with the company stating that it has already agreed to acquire the remaining 40% equity interest in Atome Financial "approximately two years after the completion of this transaction".

## FNZ CEO Blythe Masters steps down after two-year tenure

Blythe Masters is stepping down as group CEO of London-based wealth management platform FNZ after two years in the role. FNZ has appointed Stephen Welch as interim CEO and executive group chair while the company hunts for a new permanent leader.

Masters, a former JP Morgan executive, initially took over from FNZ founder Adrian Durham as CEO in August 2024. Announcing the leadership transition, FNZ says that Masters will now move to an advisory capacity for a period of six months.

The company's statement praises the "significant contribution" Masters has made during her two year tenure, namely "simplifying the group through the divestment of non-core assets" and growing on-platform assets beyond $2.5 billion.
