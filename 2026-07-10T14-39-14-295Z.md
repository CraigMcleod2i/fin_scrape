## NatWest's Wayne Freeman departs CTO role at Boxed and Mettle

Wayne Freeman has stepped down as chief technology officer (CTO) at NatWest's Banking-as-a-Service business, NatWest Boxed, and its mobile business bank account for freelancers, contractors, and small businesses, Mettle.

Announcing the news via LinkedIn on Monday, Freeman says he is assuming an advisory role until he leaves the NatWest Group at the end of August. Freeman joined the group in 2019 as CTO of Mettle, before subsequently assuming the same role at NatWest Boxed in 2023.

The outgoing exec reveals that he is being succeeded as CTO of both units by James Gawn, who has served as engineering director for Mettle and NatWest Boxed since 2023.

## Equifax to acquire Círculo de Crédito in $750m deal

Consumer credit reporting agency Equifax has agreed to acquire 100% of Mexico's Círculo de Crédito in a deal valued at $750 million.

The bureau, along with more than 1,700 clients, will become wholly owned by Equifax when the deal closes, expected in Q4 2026, subject to closing conditions and regulatory approval.

For Equifax, the acquisition expands its global footprint and presence in Mexico. Equifax CEO Mark Begor states: "Círculo de Crédito will be our 17th bolt-on acquisition in the past six years, totalling nearly $5 billion."

## Klarna applies for US banking licence

Swedish buy now, pay later (BNPL) giant Klarna has submitted applications to the Utah Department of Financial Institutions and the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) to establish Klarna Bank USA, a Utah-chartered industrial bank.

According to a company statement, the charter would enable Klarna to "bring its existing banking operations in-house, strengthening reliability across payments, savings, credit, and merchant services, and supporting sustainable growth".

US banking executive Gary Harding has been selected to serve as president and CEO of Klarna Bank USA. He has most recently served as chairman and CEO of Utah's Milestone Bank.

## Thought Machine secures £30m funding from new investor

Core banking tech provider Thought Machine has secured a £30 million funding transaction from a new investor, according to new financial accounts published this week.

The accounts state that the transaction was completed on 27 May 2026 and comprises £9 million in primary funding via the issuance of convertible loan notes alongside a £21 million secondary market transaction to facilitate the sale of employee share options.

While the identity of the backer is not disclosed in the regulatory filings, Thought Machine CEO and founder Paul Taylor told Tech.eu that the capital comes from an unnamed tier 1 bank that acts as both an investor and client.

The filings also reveal that the London-headquartered fintech quietly raised £51 million from existing shareholders in a Series E fundraising round in July 2025.

## Newgen Software appoints Tarun Nandwani as CEO

Indian tech provider Newgen Software has promoted its chief operating officer Tarun Nandwani to CEO, effective 1 August 2026.

The appointment comes following the resignation of Virender Jeet, who had been with Newgen since 1992 and served as CEO since 2021.

Nadwani began working at Newgen in 1993, and in September 2021, assumed the role of COO.

To support Nadwani and its wider growth plans, Newgen has also promoted Pramod Kumar to a newly created role, chief growth officer (CGO).
