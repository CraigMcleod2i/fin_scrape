## Bank of England sets out policy and draft rules for systemic stablecoins

The Bank of England has published a policy statement and draft Code of Practice setting out how the central bank plans to regulate systemic sterling‑denominated stablecoins used for payments in the UK. The development brings a number of targeted revisions to proposals first brought forward through consultation with the central bank in November last year.

Key revisions, published Monday, include strengthened standards for how coins are backed, including the ability for issuers to place backing funds on deposit with the central bank. "The maximum share held in interest‑bearing assets (short-term UK government debt) has been increased from 60% to 70%, with the remainder in central bank deposits," a central bank press statement reads.

Sarah Breeden, deputy governor of the Bank of England for financial stability, comments: "This is a major milestone in delivering greater choice and innovation in UK payments. Innovation thrives on trust. And today we've set out the foundations of that trust for a new form of money - with prompt redemption, strong protections and central bank support."

## Cred bags $900m investment from Meta, CEO Kunal Shah appointed to lead WhatsApp

Indian fintech unicorn Cred has secured $900 million in Series H funding from tech giant Meta. The capital will be structured through a combination of primary and secondary share purchases.

The Series H was made at a pre-money valuation of around $4.03 billion and a post-money valuation of roughly $4.5 billion.

According to a Bloomberg report, the investment sees Meta obtain a 20% stake in Cred.

Alongside the investment, Meta has announced it is also appointing Cred's founding CEO, Kunal Shah, as head of WhatsApp.

## Deluxe to acquire payments processor Celero for $625m

US payments and data company Deluxe has inked an agreement to acquire Celero Commerce in an all‑cash transaction valued at $625 million. The deal includes additional "certain seller transaction expenses", Deluxe says in a statement, and is expected to close in Q3 2026, subject to US regulatory approvals.

Celero is being sold to Deluxe by private equity firm LLR Partners, which has acted as the fintech's primary institutional backer since its inception.

The deal for Celero will enable Deluxe to "shift the revenue mix towards higher growth payments and data segments", Deluxe says. The company's statement continues: "Post-closing, the combined payments and data businesses are expected to increase to 57% of 2026 revenues on a proforma basis, compared to 31% in 2020."

## Alastair Douglas resigns as TotallyMoney CEO following Intelligent Lending buyout

Alastair Douglas has stepped down as the long-standing CEO of TotallyMoney, a credit reporting and personal finance company that is now owned by Intelligent Lending.

In a statement sent to FinTech Futures, a spokesperson for TotallyMoney said: "We'd like to thank Alastair Douglas for his contribution over the past decade at TotallyMoney."

"TotallyMoney will continue under the leadership of Nikolaos Charalampous, who is also CEO of CredAbility. Both brands sit within the same Group and will continue to operate independently, serving distinct customer needs while benefiting from shared expertise and strategic alignment," the statement continues.

## Lloyds Banking Group targets around 300 new agentic AI hires in 2026

Lloyds Banking Group in the UK is on the hunt for fresh AI talent with plans to recruit for almost 300 new positions specifically focused on agentic AI, to be filled both internally within the bank and externally.

The group cites data and AI scientists, engineers, AI product managers, and "responsible AI specialists" among its intake target.

The new hires will see Lloyds climb to more than 1,000 AI-focused roles in 2026 to support the group’s AI build-out.

AI initiatives within the group are now led by Sameer Gupta, a former DBS Bank executive who was recruited as chief data and AI officer this month.
