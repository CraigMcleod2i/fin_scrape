## Exclusive: Wallester expands to UK with EMI authorisation

Estonia-headquartered payments infrastructure fintech Wallester is preparing to launch in the UK after successfully securing authorisation as an Electronic Money Institution (EMI) with the country's Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), FinTech Futures can exclusively reveal.

The EMI application was first submitted to the regulator for Wallester UK on 1 August 2025, and received official approval on 28 May 2026. The company will use the licence to extend Wallester's embedded finance and payment infrastructure offering to enterprise clients in the region.

In an exclusive interview with FinTech Futures, Brand confirms that Wallester plans to transition from its initial build phase into an active UK market rollout later this year, with a launch pencilled in for Q3.

## Revolut co-founder Vlad Yatsenko steps down as CTO

In a major shift at the top of Revolut, Vlad Yatsenko, the UK challenger's chief technology officer (CTO) and co-founder, is stepping down from his executive role after a decade leading the company's technology operations.

Having been CEO Nik Storonsky's right-hand man since 2015, Yatsenko will be transitioning to a non-executive director position on Revolut's board from 1 July 2026.

Revolut has already lined up a successor, with Donato Lucia, the group's head of technology, set to be promoted to VP of technology, assuming Yatsenko's responsibilities.

## OpenPayd to list on Nasdaq via SPAC merger, achieving $1.1bn valuation

OpenPayd, a UK-based financial services infrastructure provider, is set to go public in the US after entering into a definitive business combination agreement with special purpose acquisition company Titan Acquisition Corp.

The transaction is anticipated to close in the fourth quarter of 2026, at which point OpenPayd will list publicly on Nasdaq and trade under the ticker symbol "OP".

The proposed transaction will value OpenPayd at unicorn status, with a pro forma equity valuation of $1.145 billion, and is expected to generate up to $276 million in gross proceeds.

## Bill restructures leadership amid 30% headcount reduction

Bill, a US-based financial automation software provider for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), has announced a series of leadership changes amid plans to reduce its workforce by 30% as it pursues its journey to becoming an AI-native company.

Writing to employees last month, René Lacerte, Bill's CEO and founder, characterised AI as "the single biggest disruptive force our industry and many others have ever seen" and stated the company must operate as a "more agile and dynamic organisation" to capitalise on this opportunity.

As a result of this shift, Lacerte announced "the hard decision to reduce our workforce by up to 30% by the end of Q4 FY2026". The CEO explained that, "going forward, we will be a flatter, leaner, faster, and more agile organisation; with our resources focused around fewer, higher impact priorities."

## Worldline completes the sale of MeTS to Magellan Partners for €400m

European payments firm Worldline has finalised the sale of its subsidiary Mobility & e-Transactional Services (MeTS) business to Magellan Partners Group, a France-based consulting and tech firm, for €400 million, with net cash proceeds valued at around €280 million.

Worldline reveals that the remaining €120 million will go towards separation costs, pension costs, other debt-like items, and capital gains tax. The firm adds that net cash proceeds also exclude approximately €40 million of cash that remains with the sold entity.

According to its sale announcement, Worldline will provide "certain technology and software services to the Magellan Partners Group to ensure business continuity during a transition period".
