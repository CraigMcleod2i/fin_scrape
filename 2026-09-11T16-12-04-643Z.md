## Chime strikes $590m all-cash deal to buy Stride Bank

US fintech Chime has agreed to purchase Oklahoma-headquartered Stride Bank for $590 million in an all-cash deal – a figure Chime claims values Stride at 1.5x its tangible book valuation.

The transaction, subject to customary closing conditions and approval by the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) and the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System, is set to be completed in H1 2027. Following this, Stride will rebrand to Chime Bank NA.

On the decision to acquire an established bank rather than apply for a de novo charter, Chime states: "The acquisition of Stride Bank provides Chime with a faster and more proven path to full-stack ownership."

"Owning the bank will eliminate partner-bank fees, reduce funding cost, and improve unit economics," Chime says. Specifically, Chime states that it "expects to realise more than $100 million in net synergies, driven by sponsor bank fee savings, expansion of lending products, and a significantly lower cost of funds".

## US Bank completes USBDC stablecoin live pilot

US Bank has officially launched its proprietary US dollar-backed stablecoin, USBDC, with the successful completion of a live cross-border pilot transaction between US Bank entities in North America and Europe.

The pilot transaction was completed on the Stellar blockchain and arrives almost a year after US Bank announced its partnership with Stellar to test custom stablecoin issuance.

US Bank will issue, manage, and move the stablecoin via its Digital Asset Platform, which connects traditional banking infrastructure and blockchain networks, with the aim to "bridge gaps in the global banking system with 24/7 transaction capabilities".

## Circle agrees $400m Tazapay acquisition to scale global USDC adoption

Circle, the issuer of the dollar-pegged USDC stablecoin, has agreed to acquire Singapore-based paytech Tazapay in a $400 million all-stock deal as it seeks to bolster its global reach.

Founded in 2020, Tazapay is a cross-border payments infrastructure provider connecting businesses to more than 100 markets and local payment methods across a variety of sectors. Stablecoins represent around 60% of the paytech's $25 billion annualised transaction volume.

Circle's acquisition will see it combine USDC with Tazapay's "banking relationships, local payout rails, and institutional customer base" to help accelerate its global adoption, says Jeremy Allaire, co-founder, CEO, and chairman of Circle.

## Lisa Jacobs to step down as CEO of Funding Circle

Lisa Jacobs has informed the board of directors at UK-based business lending platform Funding Circle that she intends to step down from her role as CEO "by no later than September 2027", the company says.

Jacobs has served as Funding Circle's CEO for the last four years having first joined the company as chief strategy officer in 2012. She held the position for seven years before transitioning to managing director (MD). Initially focused on the UK market, Jacobs quickly assumed MD responsibilities for Europe before being promoted to CEO in 2022.

The company board, chaired by Ken Stannard, has now initiated a search for a replacement. Stannard, who was appointed as chair in 2024 following the departure of Andrew Learoyd, says: "We will be very sorry to say goodbye to Lisa, but we also appreciate that 14 years is a long time. We respect her decision and how she has chosen this timing to coincide with the strong momentum of the business."

## Block submits US national trust bank charter application to OCC

US fintech Block has filed a national trust bank charter application with the OCC as it seeks to establish Builders Bank & Trust NA.

In a statement, the fintech shares that, if approved, Builders Bank will serve as an uninsured national trust bank, offering "custody and related fiduciary services, including for Bitcoin and stablecoins". Block adds that Builders Bank "would not accept deposits or make loans".

The company says that the proposed charter "would establish a federal supervisory framework for certain custody and related activities currently offered by Block".
