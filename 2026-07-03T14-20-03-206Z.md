## Japan's SBI Holdings buys Bitbank in $289m deal

Tokyo-headquartered financial conglomerate SBI Holdings has entered into an agreement to acquire domestic cryptocurrency exchange Bitbank in a deal valued at JPY 46.7 billion (approximately $288.5 million).

According to SBI, the transaction for Bitbank is expected to "enhance the Company Group’s presence in the crypto asset and digital asset fields, and further strengthen the competitiveness and profitability of its crypto asset business".

"Among domestic crypto asset exchange service providers, the Company Group is expected to rank first in terms of assets under custody and to be among the top class in terms of number of accounts," SBI continues.

The acquisition is expected to close "in or around October 2026", SBI adds, subject to approval by the Japan Fair Trade Commission. Once completed, SBI says the combined company is expected to have around JPY 1.1 trillion in assets under custody and approximately 2.92 million crypto asset accounts.

## Worldline, Mastercard, and Crédit Agricole complete France's first AI agent payment

Paris-based paytech Worldline, Mastercard, and French lender Crédit Agricole have come together to complete France's first agentic payment transaction.

The transaction's commerce flow was processed end-to-end on Worldline's infrastructure while interacting with Mastercard Agent Pay, with Crédit Agricole serving as the issuing bank to authenticate and authorise the payment.

According to a translated joint statement announcing the transaction, the initiative "demonstrates the ability to enable agentic commerce journeys within existing banking and merchant environments, while respecting market security and traceability requirements".

## Over 140 banking and tech giants team up to launch Open USD stablecoin

A cohort of major names from across the fintech and banking industries has come together through a new independent venture, Open Standard, which marks its launch this week with a new stablecoin called Open USD.

An Open Standard launch statement describes Open USD as a "new stablecoin for global money movement", while founding CEO and former Coinbase product lead Zach Abrams adds that it's "built for the internet economy, designed by the businesses growing it".

Among the 140 or so partners involved in the project are Google, Samsung, IBM, Coinbase, Solana, BlackRock, Standard Chartered, US Bank, American Express, BBVA, Visa, Mastercard, BNY, and Stripe.

The stablecoin is set to go live later this year, according to the Open Standard launch statement.

## Lloyds retires historic Halifax brand

Lloyds Banking Group has announced the retirement of the Halifax brand after 173 years, with all branches and customer accounts set to transition to Lloyds branding.

In the coming months, Halifax customers will transition to the Lloyds app and gradually see their accounts rebranded under the Lloyds name. As part of the consolidation, the Halifax brand will stop opening new accounts, though existing customers can expect business as usual.

Throughout 2027, Halifax's 190 branches will either "rebrand to Lloyds, or where there is another branch close by, customers will be served by an existing Lloyds branch in the same community", explains the UK lender.

According to Lloyds, the rebrand comes as part of its strategy to make operations across England, Wales, and Northern Ireland "simpler, smarter and more joined-up", adding that the move will allow it to "invest more in products, digital tools and personalised support".

## FCA completes crypto roadmap with publication of new rules

The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) has strengthened the UK's regulatory framework for cryptocurrency by publishing new rules for digital assets and stablecoins.

The newly updated rules, which have been introduced following a period of industry consultation, reinforce the importance of capital and stress testing cryptoassets, and put measures in place to prevent insider trading and market manipulation.

The FCA has also revealed a framework for stablecoins, introducing "simpler capital requirements for stablecoin firms" as well as tailored "trading rules to better reflect how crypto markets operate".

Specifically, the capital requirements mandate that stablecoin issuers hold 1% in funds of the total value of stablecoins they issue, as opposed to the 2% previously proposed.

The new regime will come into force on 25 October 2027, with firms requiring FCA authorisation to operate in the UK.
