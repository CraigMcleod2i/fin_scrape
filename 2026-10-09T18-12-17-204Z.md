## HSBC reportedly plans widespread UK wealth job cuts in major AI push

HSBC is planning widespread job cuts to its wealth management business in the UK in favour of more AI automation, according to the Financial Times, which cites sources with direct knowledge of the restructuring.

The cuts are due to hit approximately 70% of financial advisers and around half of managers and specialists working for the business. Departures are expected by the end of October, with HSBC reportedly consulting on the move.

A bank spokesperson tells FinTech Futures: “We’re continuing to evolve to deliver more digitally-enabled products and journeys, to support our best-in-class wealth service and meet the changing needs of our customers.”

## Mynt locks in $7bn valuation as blockbuster IPO opens to the public

GCash parent company Mynt has set its initial public offering (IPO) on the Philippine Stock Exchange (PSE) at PHP 6.60 per share in what is poised to be the country's largest listing to date.

This price per share values Mynt at approximately $7 billion, as reported by the Wall Street Journal. Mynt is looking to raise PHP 53 billion (approximately $845 million) through the sale of 8,027,409,600 common shares.

Mynt says that if the overallotment option is fully exercised, the IPO could raise up to PHP 60.9 billion (approximately $971.3 million), making it "the largest for the Philippine Stock Exchange". Banks Morgan Stanley, JP Morgan Securities, and UBS Singapore are underwriting the transaction.

## France's BPCE acquires 7% stake in Banco Sabadell

French banking group BPCE has acquired a 7% stake in Banco Sabadell, Spain's fourth-largest financial institution, through a combination of "market purchases and financial instruments", according to a joint statement. BPCE shares that it "does not intend for its shareholding in Sabadell to exceed 9.9%".

With the deal, the two banks plan to explore various "business cooperation opportunities" across several areas, including corporate and investment banking, consumer credit, equipment leasing, and cross-border clients' business. The organisations expect to finalise these discussions early next year.

The French banking group shares that it will now engage with Spanish and European regulatory authorities regarding the appointment of a representative to Sabadell's board.

## Rain submits OCC application to establish New York trust bank

Stablecoin payments infrastructure company Rain has filed an application with the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency to establish a new federal subsidiary, Rain National Trust Bank (RNTB).

In tandem with the filing, the company has named former Square Financial Services executive Brandon Soto as the proposed president and CEO of the new entity.

Rain said on Monday that, if approved, RNTB will be used solely to provide the custody, reserve management, and stablecoin issuance and redemption functions required for its partner programmes. The assets for these programmes currently sit with third-party custodians and stablecoin issuers.

## Norway's DNB to cut 400 jobs in AI-driven restructuring

Norwegian lender DNB is set to axe around 400 full-time positions as the bank shifts operations toward AI agents and digital automation. The job cuts represent nearly 4% of DNB's workforce of more than 10,500 full-time staff and will primarily impact employees within the bank's technology and services division.

The lender shares that the workforce reduction will be completed during Q4 2026, with associated restructuring costs to be recognised in the accounts during the same period.

"During the past few years, DNB has simplified a number of internal processes and routines,” according to a statement from the bank. "At the same time, the group has adopted agentic AI in several parts of its operations. This technology replaces tasks that were previously handled manually and contributes to faster processes and increased capacity, and it simplifies life for our customers."
