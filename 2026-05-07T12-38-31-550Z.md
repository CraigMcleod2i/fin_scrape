## RBI cancels banking licence issued to Paytm Payments Bank

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has cancelled Paytm Payments Bank's banking licence, according to a statement dated 24 April.

As a result, the central bank says Paytm Payments Bank "is prohibited from conducting the business of ‘banking’ as defined in Section 5(b) or any additional business specified under Section 6 of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 with immediate effect", adding: "RBI will make an application for winding up of the bank before the High Court".

The central bank assures that Paytm Payments Bank, which received a limited banking licence in August 2015, has "enough liquidity to repay its entire deposit liability upon winding up of the bank".

In its statement regarding the licence cancellation, the RBI claims that the "affairs of the bank were conducted in a manner detrimental to the interest of the bank and its depositors".

## Ebury to raise £550m in funding as Santander ups stake to 55%

UK fintech Ebury says it is raising around £550 million in a new funding initiative which will see majority shareholder Santander increase its stake in the company to 55% via a new £50 million investment.

The Spanish banking giant has served as the majority owner of Ebury since initially acquiring a controlling 50.1% stake in April 2020 for £350 million.

The funding is being split across two separate transactions, subject to customary regulatory approvals. Both transactions are being led by new investor Centerbridge Partners, with added participation from Vitruvian Partners and 83North, alongside Santander.

## Axis Bank reveals headcount reduction of around 3,100 over last year as tech investments continue

Axis Bank in India has seen its total employee headcount reduce by around 3,100 over the last 12 months amid technology-driven productivity gains from its continuing digital transformation efforts, according to Q4 FY26 results published this month.

The results count staff numbers at around 101,300 by the quarter-end, down from approximately 104,400 in Q4 FY25.

In a media conference call discussing the results for Q4 FY26, executive director Subrat Mohanty said: "The trend of headcount optimisation continues because the investments that we have made on technology over the years are starting to give us benefits in terms of productivity gains."

## Adyen to acquire loyalty platform Talon.One for €750m

Dutch paytech Adyen has signed a definitive agreement to acquire 100% of the shares in Talon.One, a Berlin-based provider of customer loyalty software, for €750 million. The transaction, which remains subject to regulatory approvals and expected to close in H2 2026, will be financed via available Adyen cash resources.

"By combining Adyen's global payments infrastructure and proprietary transaction data with Talon.One's real-time decisioning capabilities, merchants can establish a consistent customer identity across channels and apply it directly in the shopper's cart. This allows them to dynamically adjust promotions and pricing based on who the consumer is," Adyen says in a statement.

## Cater Allen names Kitty McCormick CEO as Jon Howe steps down

A CEO succession is underway at Santander UK subsidiary Cater Allen Private Bank as Jon Howe announces his departure after more than a decade of executive leadership.

In a statement posted on LinkedIn, dated 29 April, Howe writes: "As today is my final day, I wanted to take a moment to reflect on my time across Santander UK and Cater Allen Private Bank, and to thank colleagues, customers, suppliers and partners for all your support and friendship over the past 17 years."

Kitty McCormick has been confirmed as the new CEO of Cater Allen, subject to regulatory approval. McCormick has served as head of private banking at Santander UK since September 2023.

Before this, she spent nearly a decade working at NatWest Group, including nine years at Coutts, where she rose to head of innovation, followed by three years spent as director of client services at NatWest Group.
